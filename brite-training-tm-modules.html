Phase 1 — TM Pre-Training (Remote)
~1–2 weeks · Mid-July start · Self-paced digital · Ops Manager sends materials + tracks · Heaviest pre-training of any role
"The TM owns the backend": TM and AM are peer-level partners — AM owns frontend (warehouse, field, crews), TM owns backend (purchasing, reporting, recruiting, discipline, cross-entity coordination). Pre-training builds the strategic context no other role gets: company strategy, multi-entity structure, business goals. The TM makes decisions that directly affect company performance.

Phase 3 — Abbreviated Safety Certification (~half day)
Within Week 1, flexible · Warehouse + yard · Ops Manager or existing TM observes · Lighter cert — TM is managing/sales profile, workstation-based
Why abbreviated: TM works primarily from a workstation. Visits job sites periodically for quality checks and team building, but not performing installations. Abbreviated cert ensures TM can safely walk a jobsite, recognize proper safety (to evaluate crews), and handle incident reporting as territory responsible party.

No JT Base Classroom: TM track does not include the shared 3-day install/wiring/full-safety classroom that JT/ST/AM/QP/RT all attend. The TM doesn't need install-technique depth.

Phase 4 — Territory Shadowing (Weeks 2–3)
August · Existing TM territory (if available) + assigned territory · Peer TM shadowing + assigned territory orientation · Management shadowing, not field
Peer shadowing is the most valuable Phase 4 activity: Reading about the TM role is different from watching someone do it well. The existing TM's instincts — what gets checked first, when to visit an area vs. call, how to handle an iffy purchase request — can't be taught from a document. If no existing TM is available, Ops Manager runs extended Phase 2.

What's Being Evaluated 5 competencies · tracked daily
  • Backend instinct — does the TM gravitate toward workstation thinking (approvals, reporting, coordination) vs. wanting to be in the field?
  • People judgment — does the TM read candidates and existing staff well? Coach-able vs. cut-bait calls.
  • Management perspective — does the TM see the territory as a system to manage, not a set of jobs to execute?
  • Communication — clear, action-oriented, appropriate channel choice (Slack vs phone vs email vs video).
  • Cross-entity awareness — does the TM naturally consider sister-company impact, or does that stay siloed?

Phase 5 — Supervised Solo (Weeks 3–5)
Mid-August → early September · Assigned territory (remote workstation + periodic area visits) · Pre-season ramp-up · First major test
Pre-season ramp-up is the first major operational test: The TM gets the hardest management challenge (building the team from scratch) as their first solo responsibility. The timing is intentional — by the end of Phase 5, the TM should have a fully staffed, prepped territory ready for peak season.

Confirm Brite-specific targets with Ops Manager after first season of real data — handbook flags this explicitly.   • Pre-season hire date for non-Utah TMs: Mid-July works for Utah (centralized training). Out-of-state TMs may need earlier hire to allow travel for Phase 2/4 sessions in Utah, or remote Phase 2 alternatives. Decide model.

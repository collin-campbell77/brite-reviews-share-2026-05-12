Area Tier & Headcount Model

Three area tiers defined by residential production — each fixing the role roster, where positions consolidate, and the headcount math that feeds planning.
Drafted: May 29, 2026 Status: design v1 — collaboratively scoped Feeds: seasonal headcount planning (DoFO-owned)

Operating principles

Tiers = residential revenue. Classification is set by an area's residential production band — not crew count, not commercial. Crews and revenue track together; revenue is the metric.
1 AM per area. An area is the span one AM can run. Two AMs never share an area — that's two areas.
TM schedules, AM validates. The TM owns scheduling — building the job-to-crew assignments (with the AM's help). The AM is the on-the-ground check that the right jobs landed with the right crews. In consolidated tiers the AM absorbs more of this, but the schedule itself stays TM-owned.
Consolidate downward. As volume drops, seats don't vanish — they fold into fewer people (QP→RT, then everything→AM). The model names exactly where.
Commercial is a surge, not a tier. A sold commercial job initiates a protocol that "balloons" the area's capacity for that scope, then deflates. Tiers stay residential-defined.
Over-hire JTs. JT attrition is high, so hire ~1.5–2× the per-crew minimum and flex them across crews — "JTs hired" ≠ "JTs effective."
Area tier ↔ warehouse tier. T1 ≈ large-tier hub · T2 ≈ standard-tier · T3 ≈ minimal-tier — the area model and the existing warehouse buildout docs line up.

The consolidation spine. Tier 1 runs four specialist back-office seats (WA · QP · RT · LR feed). Tier 2 collapses QP into the RT (one dual-hatted person) and makes WA conditional. Tier 3 collapses everything into the AM. The RT is the pivot seat — it's the first specialist you add coming up from Tier 3, and the last you keep coming down from Tier 1.

JT counts are effective (post-attrition). Hire ~1.5–2× to hold the per-crew floor through the season. Revenue anchor: ~$5k/day/crew × ~50 working days ≈ $250k/crew/season.

Transitions & the split

  • Up / down a tier — driven by residential revenue crossing the band. The Director of Field Operations (Mike) owns the call and can pre-build a heavier headcount ahead of a season when growth is expected.
  • Commercial as catalyst — a one-off commercial job triggers the surge protocol (temporary balloon, no tier change). A major/anchor commercial contract is the usual trigger to pre-build a permanent tier-up — provision standing capacity instead of surging repeatedly.
  • The split — one AM tops out at ~5 crews; at 6, the area splits into two independently-classified areas (each its own tier, its own AM). This is the CA / FL "two AMs" case — it's two areas, never two AMs in one.

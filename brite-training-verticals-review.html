Roles & Responsibilities

TMTerritory Manager
Reports to: Operations Manager · Type: Seasonal · Volume: 1 per territory

Seasonal territory backend owner. Pairs with AM (frontend partner) — "the TM owns the backend."

Owns: scheduling — assigns jobs to crews (with the AM's help), purchasing approvals, daily/weekly/monthly reporting, recruiting + hiring, discipline + terminations, cross-entity coordination (Brite Labs, Noel Decor, Brite Supply), territory P&L, fleet oversight.

Doesn't do: day-to-day crew management, warehouse hands-on, install execution. TM reviews reports, not job sites — flags issues to AM who investigates on-site.

AMArea Manager
Reports to: TM · Type: Year-round · Volume: 1 per territory

Area operations owner — "owner-operator of the warehouse who oversees all daily execution." Being redefined from head installer to area operations manager. Year-round anchor for the area — keeps the warehouse + area productive through the off-season and opens the door to non-warehouse service lines (e.g., landscaping client properties).

Owns: schedule validation — confirms the TM has assigned the right jobs to the right crews, warehouse coordination, crew dispatch, field quality oversight, on-site client escalation, training new hires (WAs, JTs), inter-state shipment intake + airport cargo dispatch, fleet condition.

Scheduling split: the TM owns scheduling and builds the job-to-crew assignments; the AM is the on-the-ground check that the right jobs landed with the right crews — TM schedules, AM validates.

Daily cadence: 6–11 AM warehouse/office · 11–1 lunch · 1–5 field oversight · 5–6 closeout.

STSenior Field Tech / Crew Leader
Reports to: AM · Type: Seasonal · Volume: 15–20 per season

Crew leader and primary customer-facing field operator. Most STs are promoted JTs — internal promotion is the primary path.

Owns: on-site crew leadership (typically 2-man crews with a JT), wiring routing judgment ("most discrete + most efficient"), customer communication on the job, real-time JT correction in the field, ethical management compliance.

Distinct from JT: wiring routing is ST-level competency; STs have a scenario-based management assessment gate that JTs don't.

JTJunior Field Technician
Reports to: ST · Type: Seasonal · Volume: ~50 per season (highest)

Entry-level installer — the highest-volume seasonal hire. The JT track is the foundation for the other field roles; the proposed end-state is a shared JT Base Classroom (ST, AM, RT, QP joining the cohort), but today only the JT track itself has the BC-6290 restructure shipped.

Owns: install execution under ST direction — roofline, tree wrapping, greenery, hanging lights, specialty work. Learns by doing.

Distinct: JTs get classroom knowledge of wiring ("not sloppy") but routing judgment is deferred to ST. JTs progress to ST after 1+ seasons + leadership signal.

RTRepair Technician
Reports to: AM (daily 9am review) · Type: Year-round (Tier 1 only) · Volume: 1 per Tier 1 territory

"The swiss army knife." Solo repair specialist + QC + overflow installer. Trainable from scratch — first-hire is the primary path.

Owns: isolated repairs (callbacks, GFI trips, strand failures, wind damage), additional installs (greenery add-ons), quality checks in smaller territories, year-round service period coverage (Dec 12 – Jan 2 when other roles are on break).

Distinct: only role with no seasonal break besides actual holidays. Needs breadth of installation knowledge, not depth — proficiency builds through volume and variety.

QPQuality Pro Technician
Reports to: TM (primary), AM (day-to-day) · Type: Seasonal · Volume: 1–2 per territory

Solo quality inspector — "face of Brite Nites." Inspector model: incentivized to catch installer mistakes, generates quality reports per crew/ST, builds client relationships that drive 5-star reviews.

Owns: daily QP visits, 5-point crew quality scale, customer interactions (review-ask scripts), minor maintenance handling (bulbs, timers, GFI resets), repair-ticket dispatch decisions.

Escalation rule (Collin's standard): easy questions → call AM/TM direct · harder issues → repair ticket · exceptions when QP can handle it or job is far enough away that RT dispatch isn't practical.

WAWarehouse Associate
Reports to: AM (Tony in Utah HQ) · Type: Seasonal · Volume: 1 per territory (2+ at HQ)

Warehouse operations — hands-on from Day 1. Tony Gomez is the SME and primary trainer.

Owns: morning crew support (7–8 AM rush), next-day job prep (pull → section → check), shipping outbound (LTL + Southwest Cargo), product returns + cleanup, light rolling, inventory organization.

Distinct: WA does NOT do JT classroom — warehouse track is its own thing. WA scope explicitly excludes counting/audit (that's the AM via WA reports). LRs are managed by WA during pre-season + strike.

LRLight Roller contractor
Reports to: WA (training), AM (operations) · Type: Contractor (W-9) · Volume: Variable, as needed

Single-task contractor — light rolling production. Hired as needed for pre-season prep and strike season.

Owns: untangle → roll → tie → test cycle. Piece rate via Bill.com, tied to quality (bundles must pass WA Observation Checklist or they're rework).

Distinct: LR doesn't participate in counting or audit (WA scope). No remote pre-training, no formal phases, no certifications. Quality assessed through WA direct observation only.

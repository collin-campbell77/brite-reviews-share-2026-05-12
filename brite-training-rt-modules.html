Phase 1 — RT Pre-Training (Remote)
~5 days · Self-paced digital · TM sends materials, AM tracks progress · The widest pre-training scope of any role — RT needs to recognize anything that could be broken
"The diagnostic baseline": The RT must understand every installation type, every product, and every safety protocol — because any of them could be the thing that's broken. Pre-training builds the full knowledge foundation so on-site work focuses on application, not first-time exposure.

"Read these to know what right looks like." The RT reads the full install module set to understand how each install is supposed to look when done correctly. That's the diagnostic baseline — if you know what right looks like, you can spot what's wrong.
Phase 1 Gate: All 78 modules complete · digital knowledge quiz passed at 100% (unlimited retakes) · AM confirms completion + schedules first on-site day. Remote phase cannot be skipped. If not completing on time: AM follows up within 2 days. If no progress: TM reviews — may extend or terminate before on-site training.
Phase 2 — JT Base Classroom Shared
3 days · On-site · Attended with JT/ST/QP/AM cohort · For RT, the classroom is the repair lens primer · See JT Phase 2 module list
Shared classroom block: JTs/STs/QPs/AMs/RTs all attend the same 3-day classroom. For the RT, this is the install-technique foundation — every module reframed through "what does this look like when it goes wrong, and how would you fix it?" Phase 3 then layers on the RT-specific repair lens.

Full module list: View JT Base Classroom Modules →
Day 1 — Job Flow + Apps 15 modules · group classroom + hands-on
  • Game Plan walkthrough — full install job flow start to finish
  • Apps walkthrough — Crew App + BriteNow hands-on (RT's primary documentation tools)
  • RT lens: understand workflow well enough to know what's supposed to be in place when you arrive for a repair — and what's missing or wrong
Day 2 — Install Technique + Wiring + Product ~34 modules · classroom + per-task quizzes
  • Install technique detail — every Basic Skills module (24) taught in person
  • Wiring practices — 5 modules: routing, electrical, GFI, overload
  • Product detail — 5 modules: lights, strands, connectors
  • RT lens: the diagnostic baseline. RTs must know technique by name and by sight to identify what went wrong. Take same quizzes as JTs.
Day 3 — Full Safety Classroom ~27 modules · classroom + safety test
  • Ladder safety — 7 modules (A-Frame, Extension, Orchard, Little Giant, fall prevention)
  • Roof & fall protection — harness, PPE, client property protection
  • Vehicle safety — defensive driving, vehicle checks, road conditions
  • Lift safety — full MEWP classroom (11 modules) — RT does MEWP cert in Phase 4
  • Incident response — RT works solo, must own incident reporting
  • RT lens: identical safety expectation as installers. RT works solo on client property, often at height.
Phase 2 Gate (shared with JT): Day 2 per-task quizzes passed · Day 3 safety test passed at 100% · attendance + participation confirmed by AM running the classroom. RT proceeds to Phase 3 specialization next.
Phase 3 — RT Specialization Classroom (AM-Led)
~1.5 days · Office / warehouse · AM delivers · 4 RT-unique modules · Builds repair-specific skills on top of JT Base Classroom
From install to repair: Phase 2 taught what good installation looks like. Phase 3 teaches how to spot failures, how to diagnose root cause, how to communicate with a customer who just had something go wrong, and how to run a repair day as a solo operator. Every module is taught through the lens of "what went wrong, why, and how do you fix it without losing the customer?"

3B — Customer Communication shared module ~1 hour · TM-led · cross-role module
Shared / reusable module: The same Customer Communication module referenced by JT/ST/AM/TM/QP applies here. RT consumes the full shared content + an RT-specific overlay (the existing handbook brief at rt-customer-communication.md).
RT-Specific Overlay (handbook brief)
  • Arrival protocol for a repair visit — different framing than install; you're showing up because something went wrong
  • Damage assessment language — how to describe what failed, without blaming the original install (even if it's bad)
  • Timeline setting — RTs often work in fixed appointment windows; manage expectations on the spot
  • Sign-off + close-out — get explicit customer confirmation that the fix worked before leaving
  • Escalation phrases — scripted language for "I need to call my supervisor" moments (see rt-customer-communication.md#escalation-phrases)

Phase 5 — Physical Assessment + Supervised Field Work (~1 week)
Warehouse stations Day 1, then real job sites Days 2–5 · AM rides along · Progressive autonomy across the week
Apply everything on real repair appointments: First day proves physical skills at stations. The remaining 4 days are live repair work — RT does the work, AM observes and corrects. Mirrors the walk-through + 3–5 reps method, scaled up to a full week of real customer appointments.

Heights assessment (1–3) is a go/no-go decision. If the RT can't handle heights safely, the role is not a fit — TM/AM meet with the individual. RT needs to be comfortable working solo at height on client property.
Days 2–3 — Observe & Assist RT shadows AM on live repairs
  • AM handles the daily flow: 9am review, routing, vehicle loading
  • RT observes AM diagnosing and executing repairs on client properties
  • RT assists with physical work (carrying materials, holding ladders, handing tools)
  • RT practices customer introductions and explanations with AM present
  • End of each day: AM debriefs — what did you see, what would you have done differently?
Days 4–5 — Lead with Oversight RT runs full daily flow, AM rides along
  • RT runs the full daily flow: 9am review with AM → routes own appointments → loads own vehicle → executes repairs
  • AM rides along but does not intervene unless safety or quality is at risk
  • RT handles customer communication independently
  • RT submits service reports via Crew App / BriteNow
  • AM reviews every service report and provides feedback

If not passing after 5 days: up to 5 additional days extension. If still not field-ready after 10 total: TM reviews for reassignment or termination.
Phase 6 — Solo Operations (Monitored) (~2 weeks)
Territory · Independent daily operation · Daily AM 9am review is both check-in and coaching · Proves sustained performance
RT Daily Flow 5 steps · permanent operating rhythm
StepWhat Happens
9:00 AMReview the day's repair appointments with AM. Priorities, unusual situations, client history.
RoutePlan the most efficient route through the day's appointments.
LoadStock vehicle with parts and materials needed for the day's specific repairs.
ExecuteDrive to each appointment, diagnose, repair, document, move to next.
ReportSubmit service reports via Crew App / BriteNow. Flag follow-ups.
The 9am review is not training — it's how the role works. The AM reviews every service report daily and provides feedback in the next morning's 9am review. This continues permanently, not just during Phase 6.

Phase 6 Gate: 2 weeks of solo operations completed · consistent repair quality across full period · reports consistently complete + accurate · appropriate escalation decisions · no client complaints or safety incidents · AM recommends full clearance. If not meeting standard: AM extends monitored period + deepens morning review. If still underperforming after 4 weeks solo: TM reviews.
Phase 7 — Cleared (Mid-October)
Full operational status · The 9am AM review continues as a permanent part of the daily flow · Year-round role
"The 9am review continues": It's not training, it's how the role works. The AM and RT coordinate daily because the repair queue changes every day. RT clearance is full operational status — but the rhythm doesn't change.

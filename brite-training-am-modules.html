Phase 2 — Warehouse Deep-Dive (Day 1 on-site)
Full day · Warehouse (AM's home base) · Tony (Utah) or TM (satellite) · Hands-on with the person who runs the warehouse · AM does each task alongside the trainer
Why the AM learns the warehouse first: The warehouse is the operational core, and the AM must be able to manage it, troubleshoot it, train WAs, and fill in when needed. By end of Day 1, the AM should be able to run the warehouse without help. This is intentional and non-negotiable.

Phase 3 — JT Base Classroom Shared
3 days · On-site · Shared with JT/ST/QP/RT cohort · For AM, this is the install-technique foundation — they'll later co-host this classroom for crews
Shared classroom block: The single piece common to multiple role tracks. AMs sit alongside JTs/STs/QPs/RTs through the same 3-day classroom. AM lens: learning what crews learn so the AM can later teach it, evaluate it, and fill gaps in the field. AMs and STs together form the eventual "trainer bench" — AMs co-host this classroom in future seasons.

Phase 4 — Management Safety Practicals & Certifications (~1 day)
Warehouse + yard · TM observes · Practical certifications on every safety-relevant task before field immersion · No exceptions
COMPLIANCE GATE — No field work before practical sign-off. No AM enters a job site, climbs a ladder outside the warehouse, operates a lift, or works at height until practicals are passed and signed off by TM. Legal and insurance requirement.
Practical Stations 5 stations · observation pass/fail
1
Ladder practicals (all types) — setup, climb, and work on A-Frame (12'), Extension (32'), and Orchard. Pass/fail each.
2
Harness fitting + fall arrest — proper fit, anchor point identification, fall arrest demo. Pass/fail.
3
MEWP certification practical — operation, positioning, emergency descent. Pass/fail certification.
4
Lifting technique — demonstrate proper lifting form. Pass/fail.
5
Vehicle & fleet safety review — vehicle check, defensive driving demonstrated, incident reporting understood as both worker and manager.
Management Safety Scenarios management layer scored /9, minimum 6 to pass
  • Crew member refuses to use harness on a roof job — how do you respond?
  • WA has been lifting incorrectly all week — when and how do you intervene?
  • JT shows up to a job hungover — what do you do?
  • Lift operator (cleared) you supervise is making questionable judgment calls — how do you handle it?
  • Crew vehicle has visible damage — what's the protocol?
  • Crew member reports a minor injury but wants to keep working — what's the call?
  • You witness an incident on a job site — what's your sequence of actions?
  • A crew leader (ST) tells you a particular safety practice "isn't needed for this job" — how do you respond?
  • Customer complains about crew climbing on roof — how do you balance customer concern with safety reality?
Internal Promotion Path abbreviated recertification
  • Practical spot-check — TM observes one ladder type (randomly selected), harness fitting, and MEWP operation. Pass = recertified on all practical stations.
  • Full Management Safety Assessment — required regardless of entry path. This is the new competency being assessed.
  • Vehicle & Fleet Safety — required regardless of entry path. ST experience doesn't include fleet responsibility.
  • If spot-check reveals degraded skills (rusty technique after off-season), TM may require additional station recertification at their discretion.
Phase 4 Gate: All practical stations passed · Management Safety Scenarios scored ≥6/9 · TM signs off · AM cleared for Field Immersion. If not passing: immediate remediation and retesting. Safety is non-negotiable for a role responsible for the safety of others. See Phase 2: Safety Certification & Management Safety Assessment for full form.
Phase 5 — Field Immersion Week (5 days)
Field (mornings) + Warehouse/Office (afternoons) · ST crews deliver field experience + TM coaches management · Inverted schedule
Inverted schedule (intentional during immersion): Normal peak season has the AM in office/warehouse 6–10 AM then field. During immersion, the AM starts in the field to maximize crew exposure. AMs are hired before STs in the seasonal cycle — once STs are cleared, the AM shadows each ST's crew during their first working week. AM meets every crew leader 1:1 before becoming "the boss" — a rapport opportunity lost if skipped.

What the AM Does on Each Crew
  • Works with the crew — carries material, helps with installs, handles ground support. Not observing from the truck.
  • Asks questions — how does this crew handle routing? Client interactions? Warehouse needs?
  • Teaches what they know — app navigation, systems, reporting. AM comes from warehouse/management; ST comes from field. Exchange is bidirectional.
  • May step away — management calls, warehouse issues, TM check-ins take priority. AM practices splitting attention.

Phase 6 — Supervised Solo (Weeks 2–3)
Territory — warehouse, field, office · TM check-ins shift from training to coaching · "This is the guy or this isn't the guy" reveals through ability, not test
Prove-it phase: The AM operates independently through the full daily cadence. TM shifts from training to coaching — check-ins become strategic alignment, not task direction. The AM's ability to manage people, coordinate logistics, and train others is what reveals fit — not a hard pass/fail gate.

AM Strike Responsibilities
  • Coordinate takedown scheduling with TM — which properties, in what order, when
  • Oversee material recovery — returned material sorted, rolled, stored correctly
  • Inventory reconciliation — what went out vs. what came back, damage assessment
  • Manage strike crews — may differ from install crews; some seasonal employees have left
  • Warehouse transition — outbound staging → inbound receiving and storage
  • End-of-season reporting — final inventory counts, equipment condition, territory close-out
Why warehouse competency pays off in strike: AM's Phase 2 warehouse training becomes critical — must direct material recovery, verify product condition, manage transition without relying on a WA who may have left for off-season.

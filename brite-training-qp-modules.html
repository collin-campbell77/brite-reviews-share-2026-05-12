Phase 2 — JT Base Classroom Shared
3 days · On-site · AM-led · Attended with JT cohort · Also shared with ST, AM, RT · See JT Phase 2 module list
Shared classroom block: This is the single piece common to multiple role tracks. JTs, STs (external hires), AMs, RTs, and QPs all attend the same 3-day classroom together. Each role frames the content through their own lens — JTs learn execution, QPs learn evaluation, STs learn leadership.

  • Apps walkthrough — Crew App + BriteNow hands-on with real devices

Phase 3 — QP Specialization Classroom (TM-Led)
~1.5 days · Office / warehouse · TM delivers · Two modules · Builds inspection judgment + customer-facing skill on top of JT Base Classroom
From execution to evaluation: Phase 2 taught what good installation looks like by teaching how to do it. Phase 3 teaches how to evaluate it — what to look for, how to document it, and how to communicate findings to customers and crews. Every module below is taught through the lens of "what does this look like when done right, and what does it look like when done wrong?"

Role-play required: Door-knock walkthrough, 5-star review ask, complaint de-escalation, damage conversation — all role-played with TM acting as customer. QP completes at least one full pass before Phase 5.
QP Escalation Rule (Collin's standard):
• Easy questions — QP calls AM, TM, or operations lead directly. Same access pattern as crews.
• Harder issues — file a repair ticket. RT picks it up.
• Exceptions — QP can handle it themselves if capable, or if the job is far enough away that dispatching an RT isn't practical. Use judgment, document the call in the report.

Phase 5 — Field Training (AM-Led, ~1 day)
Real jobsites · AM-led walk-through then 3–5 QP reps · No modules — all live guided practice
Teaching pattern — walk-through, then 3–5 reps: AM walks through one full QP visit step-by-step — driving, narrating, doing the walkthrough conversation, completing the report. Then the QP runs 3–5 visits with AM observing and correcting in real-time. By rep 5 the QP is solo with AM in passenger seat. Same method used for any physical/practical skill in the QP track.
Field Day Structure ~1 full day · 1 AM demo + 3–5 QP reps
  • Visit 1 — AM demo: AM drives, AM inspects, AM narrates. QP observes start to finish.
  • Visits 2–3 — QP executes, AM corrects: QP drives, QP inspects, QP runs customer conversation. AM corrects in real-time before submission.
  • Visits 4–5 — QP solo with AM in passenger seat: AM intervenes only if something is going wrong.
  • Territory orientation — folded into the drive between visits; AM shows routing patterns.
  • Maintenance practice — any items found are handled by QP with AM observing (timer reset, bulb replacement, GFI reset).
  • Customer-contact reps — at least one full door-knock + walkthrough + review ask completed by QP with AM present.
Why 3–5 reps: Field experience confirms QPs internalize the workflow by the 3rd–5th visit. Fewer reps and the muscle memory isn't there; more reps without correction risks bad habits.
Phase 5 Gate: Field Readiness Evaluation — 3–5 visits completed (1 AM demo + 2–4 QP reps minimum) · all service reports reviewed and approved · at least one customer interaction completed successfully · AM confirms independent operation, reports readiness to TM.
Phase 6 — Supervised Solo (~2–3 days)
Field — solo · TM reviews every report end-of-day · Feedback loop tight: correction immediately, not after habit forms
Solo Operation Independent — full daily workflow
  • Solo QP visits — full daily workflow: coordinate with TM/AM, schedule visits, contact customers, execute inspections, complete reports
  • TM report review (every report) — completeness, photo quality, ranking accuracy, issue identification
  • End-of-day feedback — TM provides feedback on report quality, missed items, client interaction
  • Customer-interaction quality — TM samples 1–2 customers (text or call) to verify QP's tone matched the standard
Phase 6 Gate: Solo Performance Review — 2+ full solo days with no recurring quality issues · TM confirms report quality meets standard · inspection accuracy (no recurring missed items) · customer interactions professional and generating review requests · TM confirms readiness for clearance.
Phase 7 — Cleared (End of Week 4)
Formal TM sign-off · Spot-check rhythm continues · QP is full team member through end of season
Clearance Criteria — TM's Standard
  • Pre-training + classroom complete — Phase 1 modules, Phase 2 safety test, Phase 3 inspection knowledge check, Phase 4 ladder cert all confirmed
  • Inspection judgment — identifies installer mistakes consistently; ranks crews with reasoning that holds up
  • Report quality — complete, accurate, actionable; TM relies on QP reports without follow-up
  • Customer-facing skill — 5-star reviews being generated; complaints stay professional and de-escalate
  • Independence — runs full daily workflow without direction beyond the schedule
  • Safety — uses Little Giant correctly every time; defensive driving practiced; no shortcuts
Progression Path
  • Most common next role: Repair Technician — similar solo/diagnostic workstyle, expands troubleshooting depth
  • Returning season: Veteran QPs may absorb additional territory or train new QPs as TM extension
CLEARED — Quality Pro Technician: all Phase 1–6 gates passed · safety certs on file · TM formal sign-off. After clearance, TM continues reviewing QP reports — shifts from every-report review to spot-checking. QP failures are documented and flagged to follow the initial installer, generating quality reports per crew/ST that the TM shares with AMs for corrections.
